HIGHLIGHTS                                                GRAPHICAL                ABSTRACT

• A curvature approximation method to
  measure the rate of capacity fade is pro-
  posed.
• A new oscillatory degradation phenomenon
  is discovered.
• The oscillatory degradation phenomenon
  is used to identify knee and knee-onset.
• The method outperforms the state-of-
  the-art method on both synthetic and
  real data.
• The method can be used for online knee-
  onset identification and knee prediction.




ARTICLE                 INFO                              ABSTRACT

Keywords:                                                 Abrupt capacity fade can have a significant impact on performance and safety in battery applications. To
Battery diagnosis                                         address concerns arising from possible knee occurrence, this work aims for a better understanding of their
Degradation mechanisms                                    cause by introducing a new definition of capacity knees and their onset. A curvature-based identification of
Knee identification
                                                          a knee and its onset is proposed, which relies on the discovery of a distinctly fluctuating behavior in the
Knee-onset early prediction
                                                          transition between an initial and a final stable acceleration of the degradation. The method is validated on
Curvature approximation
                                                          experimental degradation data of two different battery chemistries, synthetic degradation data, and is also
                                                          benchmarked to the state-of-the-art knee identification method in the literature. The results demonstrate that
                                                          our proposed method could successfully identify capacity knees when the state-of-the-art knee identification
                                                          method failed. Furthermore, a significantly strong correlation is found between knee and end of life (EoL)
                                                          and almost equally strong between knee onset and EoL. As the method does not require the full capacity fade
                                                          curve, this opens up online knee-onset identification as well as knee and EoL prediction.
