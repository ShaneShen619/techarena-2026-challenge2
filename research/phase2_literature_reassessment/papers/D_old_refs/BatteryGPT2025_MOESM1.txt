ABSTRACT




Supplementary Note 1: Description of Datasets
1) MIT Dataset:     The aging data from MIT1 is derived from lithium iron phosphate (LFP)/graphite batteries. The
experimental conditions involved a forced convection temperature chamber set at 30◦C. The batteries have a nominal
capacity of 1.1Ah and a nominal voltage of 3.3V.
    All batteries in this dataset undergo a two-step fast charging strategy. The format of this strategy is expressed as
"C1(Q1) −C2", where C1 and C2 represent the charging rate for the first and second constant current steps, and Q1
denotes the current switch charging step.
    The first current step concludes at Q1 state of charge (SOC), and the second current step concludes at 80% SOC,
after which the battery undergoes a 1C constant current-constant voltage (CC-CV) charging until reaching the upper
cutoff voltage. Afterwards, maintain a constant current discharge until reaching the lower cutoff voltage. The upper and
lower cutoff potentials are specified as 3.6V and 2.0V, respectively.

Supplementary Note 2: Description of Deep Learning Methods
1) Convolutional Neural Network:        Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are a type of deep learning model that
excel in processing image data. Their architecture, inspired by the human visual cortex, is particularly effective in
computer vision tasks such as image classification, object detection, and image segmentation. This makes CNNs a
powerful tool not only for general image processing but also for specialized applications like predicting the SOH and
EOL of batteries2 .
    When applied to battery SOH and EOL prediction, CNNs can analyze various types of data, often converting
non-image data into formats that resemble images. In this work, a CNN could process time-series data from a battery’s
usage history, converting this data into a 2D format akin to an image. This "image" could then be analyzed to identify
patterns that are indicative of the SOH and expected lifespan of the battery3 .
    The CNN model, with its layers of convolutional filters, excels in identifying and extracting features from these
data representations. In the context of early-stage SOH prediction, these features include charge-discharge cycles,
temperature variations and voltage behaviors. By learning from historical data of batteries full lifecycle and health
degradation are known, a CNN can learn to predict similar outcomes for new batteries under observation.

2) Long Short-Term Memory: Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTMs) are a type of recurrent neural network
(RNN) particularly well-suited for processing and making predictions based on time-series data. LSTMs are designed
to overcome the limitations of traditional RNNs, such as the difficulty in learning long-range dependencies in sequences.
This makes LSTMs highly effective for tasks involving sequential data, such as natural language processing, speech
recognition, and the early-stage battery SOH prediction4, 5 .
    When applied to battery SOH and EOL prediction, LSTMs can process time-series data that reflects a power usage
and condition over time. This data can include metrics like charge-discharge cycles and temperature variations that
influence the SOH changes.
    The key strength of LSTMs in this context lies in their ability to remember and utilize long-term dependencies in
the data. For instance, they can recognize patterns and trends over extended periods, which are critical for predicting
the SOH of batteries. This capability is essential for accurately forecasting how a battery will degrade under various
usage conditions and environmental factors.
3) CNN and LSTM fusion model:         Convolutional LSTM (Conv-LSTM)6 networks are an innovative integration of
CNNs and Long LSTM networks. This hybrid model combines the spatial feature extraction capabilities of CNNs with
the temporal sequence modeling strengths of LSTMs, making it particularly effective for tasks involving both spatial
and temporal data.
    In the domain of battery SOH and EOL prediction, Conv-LSTMs can effectively process and analyze data that is
both spatial and temporal in nature. In this work, a Conv-LSTM could be used to analyze time-series data from the
power usage history, along with spatial data that could be related to the internal structure or condition of the battery
cells. This include data on charge-discharge cycles, temperature variations and voltage behaviors in this work.
4) Transformer:    Transformers7 are a type of deep learning model that have revolutionized the field of natural language
processing (NLP) and have significant potential in various other domains, including time-series data analysis for
applications like predicting SOH and EOL of batteries. Unlike traditional models that process data sequentially,
Transformers utilize a mechanism called "attention" to weigh the significance of different parts of the input data,
allowing for parallel processing and a more nuanced understanding of data relationships.
    In the context of battery SOH and EOL prediction, Transformers can analyze time-series data from a power usage
history, including charge-discharge cycles, temperature variations, voltage behaviors, and other relevant parameters7 .
The model’s attention mechanism allows it to focus on specific parts of the data that are most relevant for predicting the
SOH and EOL, potentially identifying complex patterns and relationships that other models might miss. One of the
key advantages of Transformers in this application is their ability to handle long-range dependencies in the data. This
means they can effectively relate and interpret data points that are distant from each other in time.

Supplementary Note 3: Training Hyperparameters and Training Process of Deep
Learning Models
In the regression prediction method, the input to the model are the charging features corresponding to three historical
charge/discharge cycles. The shape of the input data varies depending on the method used. The output to the model is
state of health (SOH) corresponding to the adjacent next charge/discharge cycle. The regression prediction methods
used in this paper are Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Long Short-Term Memory Networks (LSTM), and
CNN-LSTM Fusion Networks. The first 42 battery cells are used as the training set and the rest as the validation set.
The parameters of the model are saved every fifty epochs, The maximum epoch number is set to 10000, and the model
parameters with the lowest validation set loss are selected for validating the predictive performance of the model. All
regression prediction methods use the same training parameters, the data in the training set is shuffled with a batch size
of 6. and the initialised learning rate is 10e4, using the Adaptive Moment Estimation (Adam) optimiser. are trained
based on a single NVIDIA RTX4090.
1) CNN:     Specifically, the features of each charge/discharge cycle are reconstructed into a three-channel feature
map of length and width both 60, where each channel corresponds to voltage, current and temperature, respectively.
Subsequently, the feature maps corresponding to three historical charge/discharge cycles are stacked together to form
the input data to the CNN. Such a shape of the input data helps to preserve the time-series information of battery aging
and allows the CNN to efficiently capture the time dependence. CNN for regression prediction consist of several key
components:




                                                                                                                             2/12
    • Convolutional layers: two convolutional layers are used in this paper and these layers are used to extract aging
      features at different levels. The first convolutional layer is responsible for capturing the correlation between
      various battery features in each charge-discharge cycle. The number of input channels is 3, corresponding to
      the channels of the reconstructed feature map, the number of output channels is 1, the convolution kernel size
      is (3,3), the stride is 1 and the padding is 1. The output feature matrix of the first convolutional layer is of the
      same length and width as the input feature map, and the number of channels is 1. The feature matrices of the
      three charge/discharge cycles are stacked to form a new three-channel feature map as the input to the second
      convolutional layer to capture temporal correlations between the three charge/discharge cycles and deeper aging
      features. The number of input channels is 3, corresponding to the number of historical charge/discharge cycles.
      The rest of the convolution parameters are the same as in the first convolutional layer.
    • Flatten Layer: the output of the convolutional layers is passed through the flatten layer to transform the multidi-
      mensional data into one-dimensional vectors for input to the linear layer.
    • Linear layers: the CNN model used for regression prediction contains two linear layers. The first linear layer has
      an input feature dimension of 3600 and an output feature dimension of 100 while the second linear layer has an
      input feature dimension of 100 and an output feature dimension of 1. These linear layers are used to generate the
      final SOH predictions.

2) LSTM:     Each charge/discharge cycle generates a large amount of battery features that encompasses the electrochem-
ical and physical properties of the battery, as well as the effects of ageing. However, due to the large number of features,
placing all the features of a charge/discharge cycle in a single time node to use the more interpretable LSTM structure
may face computational and parametric problems. To overcome this problem, in this paper, battery features from three
historical charge/discharge cycles are reconstructed into a feature sequence of length 20, where each time step of the
sequence is a set of 1620-dimensional features, and such a sequence was fed into the LSTM model. The LSTM model
consists of the following key components:

    • LSTM layers: this paper uses three-layer LSTM, each with a hidden layer dimension of 25. This allows the
      model to capture temporal dependencies and sequence patterns at multiple levels of abstraction. The number
      of input features for each layer is 1620, which is the number of features in the reconstructed charging feature
      sequence.
    • Flatten layer: the output of the LSTM layer is flattened into a one-dimensional vector. This step is done to convert
      the output of the LSTM into a shape that fits the input of the linear layer.
    • Linear layer: the flatten feature vectors are fed into a linear layer which has an input feature dimension of 500
      and an output feature dimension of 1. This linear layer is used to generate the final SOH predictions.

3) CNN-LSTM:       The input to the CNN-LSTM network is the same as the CNN described in the previous section,
in which the features of each charge/discharge cycle are reconstructed into a three-channel feature map according to
the same pattern. This type of data processing helps to preserve the time-series information and the dynamics of the
battery features. However, instead of using a flatten layer, CNN-LSTM reconstructs the aged features obtained through
two convolutional layers into a feature sequence of length 20, which preserves the temporal structure of the data and
passes it into the LSTM layer for further temporal modelling. The CNN-LSTM model consists of the following key
components:

    • Convolutional layer: the convolutional layer hyperparameters of CNN-LSTM networks are the same as those of
      CNN networks, which are two layers of convolutional layers.
    • LSTM layer: since the input data of the LSTM layer is changed, the number of input features of the LSTM layer
      is 180, and the other parameters of the LSTM layer are set the same as the previous LSTM layer.
    • Flatten layer: the output of the LSTM layer is flattened into a one-dimensional vector. This step is done to convert
      the output of the LSTM into a shape that fits the input of the linear layer.
    • Linear layer: the flatten feature vectors are fed into a linear layer which has an input feature dimension of 500
      and an output feature dimension of 1. This linear layer is used to generate the final SOH predictions.

Supplementary Note 4: Training parameters and training process of the self-regression
model
The autoregressive prediction method is divided into two main modules according to the function. The first module uses
the time-series features of early-stage prediction starting offset (EPSO) as input to predict the time-series features of
full lifecycle. The second module takes the features of each cycle as input and estimates the SOH of the corresponding
cycle. Therefore, the autoregressive prediction model takes the EPSO’s time-series features as input to obtain the SOH
prediction results of full lifecycle. In this paper, the autoregressive prediction model is discussed in the following
sections.

Time-series features prediction module
The time-series features of EPSO are used as input to the time-series features prediction module. The inputs are turned
into time-series tokens of EPSO after tokenization. The vocabulary size of each token is the same as BatteryGPT,
which is 743. The output of the time-series features prediction module is the next token of the time-series, which is
reconverted to the next feature of the time-series after the opposite tokenization rule. The first 42 battery cells are used
as the training set and the rest as the validation set. The training is terminated when the autoregressive error, root mean
square error, of the validation and training sets is less than 5% or the epoch number reaches 500000. The minimum
epoch number is set to 1000. All time-series features prediction modules in this paper are using the Adaptive Moment
Estimation (Adam) optimizer and are trained based on a single NVIDIA RTX4090.
LSTM
In this paper, time-series features represent continually gathered battery features over a specific period, typically
reflecting the evolving trends of battery properties. LSTM can analyze historical battery performance feature to predict
the battery’s feature at future time points. The initialized learning rate of the LSTM is 10-4, as the number of epochs
increases, the learning rate will decrease to 10-5 at the 4000th epoch. All input time-series features are divided by a
window of length 256 with a batch size of 64. The LSTM for time-series features prediction consists of the following
key components:

    • Embedding layer: the main role of this layer is to embed tokens into multidimensional features, conforming to the
      input of the LSTM layer. In this paper, vocabulary size is 743, and dimension of output embedding is set to 128.
    • LSTM layer: the number of input features is 128, the hidden layer dimension is 2000, and the number of layers
      in the LSTM layer is 6.

    • Flatten layer: the output of the LSTM layer is flattened into a one-dimensional vector. This step is done to convert
      the output of the LSTM into a shape that fits the input of the linear layer.
    • Linear layer: the dimension of input features is 512000 and the dimension of output features is 743, which is
      same as vocabulary size.
    • Classification layer: sampling from a probability distribution via softmax and Multinomial Distribution (MD) to
      determine the next feature to be generated.
Transformer
The initialized learning rate of the Transformer is 10-6, as the number of epochs increases, the learning rate will
decrease to 10-7 at the 4000th epoch. All input time-series features are divided by a window of length 256 with a batch
size of 2. The Transformer for time-series features prediction consists of the following key components:

    • Embedding layer: the main role of this layer is to embed tokens into multidimensional features, conforming to
      the input of the Transforemer layer. In this paper, vocabulary size is 743, and dimension of output embedding is
      set to 128.
    • Transformer layer: The number of input features is 1024, the number of layers for encoder and decoder is the
      same, both are 16, and the dimension of the hidden layer of the feedforward neural network (FFN) is 10242
      = 1048576. The role of FFN is to introduce non-linear mappings, enabling the model to learn more complex
      functional relationships. This helps enhance the model’s representation capabilities of input data, allowing it to
      better capture patterns and features within input sequences.

    • Linear layer: the dimension of input features is 512000 and the dimension of output features is 743, which is
      same as vocabulary size.
    • Classification layer: sampling from a probability distribution via softmax and Multinomial Distribution (MD) to
      determine the next feature to be generated.

SOH Estimation Module
In the SOH estimation module, the input data to the module are the charging features corresponding to a single
charge/discharge cycle, and the shape of the input will vary depending on the method used. The output of the model is
the SOH corresponding to the corresponding charge/discharge cycle. The first 42 battery cells are used as the training
set and the rest as the validation set. The model’s parameters are saved every fifty epochs, and the training process
is set to run for a maximum of 10,000 epochs. The model parameters associated with the lowest validation set loss
are chosen to assess the predictive performance of the model during validation. The estimation methods all use the
same training parameters, the data in the training set is shuffled with a batch size of 6. The initial learning rate for all
estimation methods is set to 0.0001, and the Adam optimizer is utilized across all methods. Training is conducted on a
single NVIDIA RTX4090.
CNN
The features of each charge-discharge cycle are reconstructed into three-channel feature maps with a length and width
both set to 60. Each channel corresponds to voltage, current, and temperature, respectively. CNN for regression
prediction consist of several key components:
    • Convolutional layers: two convolutional layers are used in this paper and these layers are used to extract aging
      features at different levels. The number of input channels of the first convolutional layer is 3, the number of
      output channels is 6, the convolutional kernel size is (3,3), the step size is 1 and the padding is 1. The number
      of input channels of the second convolutional layer is 6, the number of output channels is 1, and the rest of the
      parameters are the same.
    • Flatten layer: the output of the convolutional layer is flattened into a one-dimensional vector to fit the input of the
      linear layer.
    • Linear layers: the CNN model used for SOH estimation contains two linear layers. The first linear layer has an
      input feature dimension of 3600 and an output feature dimension of 100 while the second linear layer has an
      input feature dimension of 100 and an output feature dimension of 1. These linear layers are used to generate the
      final SOH prediction.
LSTM
In this paper, battery features are reconstructed into a feature sequence of length 20, where each time step of the
sequence is a set of 540-dimensional features, and such a sequence was fed into the LSTM model. The LSTM model
consists of the following key components:

    • LSTM layers: this paper uses three-layer LSTM, each with a hidden layer dimension of 25. The number of input
      features for each layer is 420, which is the number of features in the reconstructed charging feature sequence.
    • Flatten layer: the output of the LSTM layer is flattened into a one-dimensional vector. This step is done to convert
      the output of the LSTM into a shape that fits the input of the linear layer.
    • Linear layer: the flatten feature vectors are fed into a linear layer which has an input feature dimension of 500
      and an output feature dimension of 1.

CNN-LSTM
In this paper a fusion network structure is introduced that combines CNN with LSTM network to deal with battery
features. The input to the CNN-LSTM network is the same as the CNN described in the previous section, in which the
features of each charge/discharge cycle are reconstructed into a three-channel feature map according to the same pattern.
However, instead of using a flatten layer, CNN-LSTM reconstructs the aged features obtained through two convolutional
layers into a feature sequence of length 20, which preserves the temporal structure of the data and passes it into the
LSTM layer for further temporal modelling. The CNN-LSTM model consists of the following key components:

      CNN networks, which are two layers of convolutional layers.
    • LSTM layer: since the input data of the LSTM layer is changed, the number of input features of the LSTM layer
      is 180, and the other parameters of the LSTM layer are set the same as the previous LSTM layer.

    • Flatten layer: the output of the LSTM layer is flattened into a one-dimensional vector. This step is done to convert

    • Linear layer: the flatten feature vectors are fed into a linear layer which has an input feature dimension of 500

Multilayer Perceptron
The Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) is a type of feedforward neural network composed of one or multiple hidden layers,
with each hidden layer containing multiple neural nodes. MLP is widely used in machine learning tasks such as
classification and regression. In this paper, the SOH is estimated by inputting the battery features after flattening them
into a two-layer MLP with an input layer dimension of 10800, a hidden layer dimension of 150, and an output layer
dimension of 1.

Supplementary Note 5: Hyperparameter Optimization of BatteryGPT
Hyperparameter Optimization (HPO) is a crucial step in the training of machine learning models. In the context of
machine learning, hyperparameters are external configuration settings for a model that are not learned from the data
but must be specified before the training process begins. Examples include learning rates, regularization strengths,
and the number of hidden layers in a neural network. The goal of hyperparameter optimization is to find the optimal
set of hyperparameters that maximizes the performance or accuracy of a model on a specific task. There are various
techniques for hyperparameter optimization, including grid search, random search, and more sophisticated algorithms
such as Bayesian optimization and genetic algorithms. These methods systematically explore the hyperparameter space
to identify the combination that leads to the best model performance.
    Due to the limitation of the experimental equipment, this paper uses the method of grid search to find the optimal
hyperparameters. The search hyperparameter space is as follows:
    batch_size: Represents the number of samples in each training batch, with only one choice, which is 4.
    attn_drop_ratio: Represents the dropout ratio in the attention mechanism, with three choices: 0.5, 0.25, and 0.1.
    block_size: Represents the number of previous characters in each input sequence, with three choices: 256, 512, and
1024.
    depth: Represents the depth of the decoder, with only one choice, which is 5.
    embedding: Represents the size of the embedding layer, with three choices: 384, 768, and 1024.
    Learning_rate: Represents the learning rate taken during the learning process, with two choices: 1e-3 and 1e-4.

Supplementary Note 6: Evaluation on the KIT Dataset
The KIT dataset8 is introduced to assess the external validity of BatteryGPT beyond the MIT benchmark used in the
main text. KIT contains long-horizon cycling under EV-like operating profiles, multiple ambient temperatures, and
pronounced accelerated-degradation knees. This note summarizes the evaluation protocol, baselines, metrics, and
results on KIT, and explains why they substantiate the claims made for BatteryGPT.
    Dataset and preprocessing Cells are cycled over long durations with periodic check-ups and temperature variation.
We adopt the same tokenization scheme used for MIT: voltage, current, and temperature are discretized into non-
overlapping vocabularies with fixed offsets; per-dataset numeric ranges are estimated from the training split with a small
safety buffer. All experiments use chronological splits. The SOH estimator operates on short windows of generated
tokens, identical to the design described in the main text.
    Evaluation protocol BatteryGPT is prompted with the initial portion of a life and then run in a free-running, fully
autoregressive mode to the end of life. We report two prompt lengths: BatteryGPT(5) and BatteryGPT(30), where the
number denotes the number of observed cycles provided as the prompt before free generation. This protocol matches
the early-prediction use case. For interpretability and the missing-data analysis, we also consider predictive starting
offsets (EPSO) as illustrated in Supplementary Fig. S5: with a fixed prompt length (256 tokens), the model can start at
5%, 10%, or 30% of life and generate the remainder; this is equivalent to late starts or long missing prefixes.
