Huawei Tech Arena 2026 – Grid-Forming BESS Battery Lifetime Early Prediction
Challenge 1: Predicting Battery Lifetime
1. The Task
Whether open battery data plus a handful of in-house tests can replace years of aging is one of the most practical open
questions in battery development. Your task: pre-train an aging model on open-source LFP datasets, fine-tune it with the small
provided dataset of our prismatic 102 Ah LFP cell, and predict its aging trajectory down to 70% SOH, including operating
conditions the model have not seen before. If the test condition does not provide SOH down to 70%, the last available SOH can
be counted for testing and validation. Only 102 Ah predictions are scored, for this a hold-out dataset will be used, that will not
provided to you. The SOH throughout the challenge is considered as the full available discharge capacity divided by the
nominal capacity.
   1. Access and process. You receive a dataset containing different aging conditions of a 102 Ah LFP cell. This dataset is the
      target data, where the final validation and reporting needs to be done from your side. Get familiar with the dataset.
      The data contains some challenges that need to be handled, like missing data or outliers. In addition, there is a list of
      links to public LFP aging datasets that can be used to pre-train your model.
   2. Pre-train on open data. Build your pre-training corpus from the linked datasets and optionally any additional publicly
      available.
   3. Fine-tune on target data. Adapt your model to the 102 Ah cell using only the provided target data set. You can utilize
      different techniques, like transfer learning, domain adaptation, few-shot methods, physics-informed constraints, etc. –
      your choice.
   4. Predict to end of life. The overall goal is to predict SOH over cycle number down to 70% SOH, including the knee-point,
      for the 102 Ah cell, the data was provided for, within the following operating boundaries: from 25 to 55 °C and from
      0.5C to 1C.
   5. Do more with less (bonus). Every submission is additionally evaluated in a reduced-budget run, in which the pipeline
      provides only a subset of the fine-tuning data (fewer cells and early-life cycles only; exact budget in the Submission &
      Validation Guide). Models that keep their accuracy with less proprietary data earn a data-efficiency bonus on top of
      their score.
   6. Package and submit as often as you like. Standardized command-line interface and output CSV (Submission &
      Validation Guide will be shared during the Webinar); a public leaderboard will be generated; only submissions that run
      successfully through the automated pipeline are scored.
Given: (a) a list of public LFP aging datasets for pre-training, (b) the target data set of the 102 Ah cell: with different aging
conditions
Wanted: for every condition of the full test matrix, the predicted SOH-over-cycle trajectory from cycle 1 down to 70% SOH
including the knee-point, one trajectory per evaluation cell for the fine-tuning conditions (the withheld sibling cells), and one
trajectory per condition for the six unseen conditions. Predictions are evaluated against a hidden ground truth of cells cycled
deep through the knee and, at several conditions.
Relevance of the problem: the transfer is hard on every axis. Public LFP datasets are dominated by small cylindrical cells under
fast-charge protocols and different voltage windows; our product cell is a 102 Ah prismatic format under symmetric CC-CV
cycling. Formats differ, thermal behaviour differs, knee mechanisms may differ. A naive model transfers noise; a good one
transfers physics. Whether open data plus minimal proprietary testing can predict the lifetime of a new cell is a genuinely open
research question and a relevant question for product development.

2. Data
2.1 Open-source pre-training data
A list of public LFP aging datasets is provided. Participants may additionally use any other battery dataset that satisfies the
following two rules: (1) documented with citation and license in the final report; (2) reproducible by the pipeline (download
scripts or declared cached copies). Private or proprietary external data is not allowed. Performance on open data is not scored.

2.2 The 102 Ah fine-tuning set (provided as download)
Every cell was aged under different operating conditions and the log data are provided as CSV files, per cell (cell_id =
102Ah_<T>degC_<rate>_cell<n>).

               File                                                              Content (one row per …)
                                    30 s record: cycle_number, step_type (rest / cccv_charge / cc_charge / cc_discharge / other), time_in_cycle_s,
 <cell_id>_time_series_partNN.csv   voltage_V, current_A, temperature_C (empty where no valid sensor), step_capacity_Ah, absolute_time. Split at cycle
                                    boundaries into numbered parts of at most 100 MB each; concatenating the parts in order restores the full series.

Protocol: continuous symmetric full cycling at fixed ambient temperature — 30 min rest, CC-CV charge to 3.649 V, 30 min rest, CC discharge to
2.5 V; 30 s sampling; voltage window 2.5–3.65 V. Real lab data means real imperfections: partial boundary cycles are flagged (not silently
dropped), verification-protocol segments are marked logic_test, and temperature availability varies by cell.
